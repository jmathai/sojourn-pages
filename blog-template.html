Reading

On envy, and the long work of loving what is not ours

By A. Writer · 6 min read

¶Written by a human, without any AI assistance.

This opening paragraph sets the tone. Body text is set large in a serif face on a warm paper ground, at a comfortable measure of about seventy characters, so a reader can move through it without strain. Keep sentences plain and unhurried.

A second paragraph can carry a bolded emphasis, an italic aside, or an inline link to another passage or source. Use them sparingly.

A section heading

Sections break the piece into readable movements. Introduce the idea in prose before showing anything set apart from the text.

1 Corinthians 13:4–5 4Love is patient and kind. Love does not envy. Love does not brag, is not proud, 5does not dishonor others, is not self-seeking, is not easily angered, keeps no record of wrongs.

After a citation, return to prose to reflect on it. The verse numbers are optional; drop the small <span class="v"> markers for plain quotation. Wrap a phrase in <em> inside a citation to give it the amber highlight.

A smaller subheading

Use a third-level heading for a point within a section. Lists work for enumerated thoughts:

  • An unordered point, kept short.
  • A second point that continues the thought.
  • A third, if the rhythm of three feels right.
  1. An ordered step, when sequence matters.
  2. The step that follows it.

A pull quote lifts a line of your own writing, or another writer's, out of the flow so a reader pauses on it.

Attribution, if any

Inline code such as faith or a variable is set in a mono face on a tinted chip. For longer code, use a block:

function greet(name) {
  return `Peace to you, ${name}.`;
}

greet("reader");

Close with a final paragraph that lands the piece quietly, without a hard sell.

A short footnote, colophon, or further-reading line can sit here in the sans face.
