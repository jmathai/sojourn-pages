Topic

Obedience

What scripture knows about the love that keeps its word.

Obedience is a word most of us flinch at. It sounds like a boot on the neck, like doing a thing because someone bigger said so. We spend our whole childhood trying to get out from under it, and here is scripture asking for it all over again, from God this time.

But watch where the Bible actually sets the word down. It almost never stands alone. It comes tangled up with love, with trust, with knowing someone well enough to follow them into the dark. The call to obey God arrives right on the heels of the call to love him, as though the two were one motion seen from different sides.

That changes what the word even means. Obedience stops being the price you pay for approval and becomes the shape your trust takes once it finally moves. You can say you trust someone all day long. Obedience is trust that got up and did something about it.

Scripture is honest that this is hard, and that now and then it costs everything. It never pretends otherwise. But it keeps insisting the commands were never meant to crush you. They run with how you were made, not against it.

This study walks from obedience as duty toward obedience as love. Read it slowly. Every verse below is a door. Touch one and it opens into the chapter it came from, so you can stand in the whole room a single line was lifted from.

The study

Start with what obedience is not. King Saul had been given a hard command and carried out most of it, holding back the best of what he was told to destroy, meaning to make a grand sacrifice of it later. It looked like devotion. The prophet Samuel named it for what it was.

To obey is better than sacrifice. God is not after the display of religion, the impressive offering, the big gesture with an audience. He is after your listening. Obedience isn’t the heavy, dutiful thing we brace for. It’s what listening looks like once you trust the voice.

So if obedience is what God is after, where is it meant to come from? The oldest answer in scripture, the words Israel was told to teach their children and speak at the door of the house, roots it in one place before it asks for a single thing.

Love first. The commandments come next, and they land on a heart already told to love. That order is everything. Obedience that begins anywhere but love curdles into fear or performance every time. Here it is meant to be love with somewhere to go.

Jesus takes that ancient link and says it plainly, about as simply as it can be put.

He says it again a few lines on, and hangs a promise on the other side of it, “One who has my commandments and keeps them, that person is one who loves me” John 14:21, and then once more, “If a man loves me, he will keep my word” John 14:23. Notice he never makes obedience the way to earn his love. He makes it the evidence that the love is already there. Keeping his word is not how you get in. It is what being in looks like from the outside.

And in case obedience still sounds like something for lesser creatures, scripture sets the highest name it has underneath the word. The model is not a servant or a child. It is Christ, choosing to come down.

Obedient to the point of death. The one with every right to be served instead did the thing he was sent to do, all the way to the worst of it. Whatever obedience is, it cannot be small or servile, because this is where it led the person scripture holds highest of all. He did not obey because he was beneath his Father. He obeyed because he trusted him, clear into the dark.

It would be easy to hear all of this as a heavier and heavier load. Scripture cuts that off before it can start. It flatly refuses to let obedience be recast as a burden.

His commandments are not grievous. Not because they ask little of you, but because they are not a tyrant’s demands for what he is owed. They are the instructions of the one who made you and knows the way you run best. Less a fence than a trellis, the frame that lets a vine climb instead of sprawl along the ground.

Jesus ends his great sermon not on a feeling but on a test, and the test is doing. Two people hear the very same words. Only one of them is changed by it, and you cannot tell which until the weather comes.

Both men heard. That is the sobering part. Hearing was never the thing that held. The house on the rock is not the one with the best theology or the strongest feelings about God. It is the one whose owner did what he heard. This is where the whole study has been walking. Obedience is just love, and trust, with their hands finally in the work, building something that stands when the rain comes.

The conversation

One way a conversation about obedience can go in Sojourn. The verses are live, the same doors as above.

I grew up being taught that being a Christian was mostly about following the rules, and it left me feeling like God was a boss I could never quite please. If we’re saved by grace, why does the Bible still push obedience so hard?

Because in scripture obedience was never the bill you pay for God’s love. It is what having that love looks like from the outside. Jesus put the two in the order most of us grew up hearing backwards, “If you love me, keep my commandments” John 14:15. Love is the root; keeping his word is the fruit, not the entrance fee. It is why John can call the commands light in the same breath as calling them love, “His commandments are not grievous” 1 John 5:3. Grace really does mean you can stop earning. It never meant the love it hands you just sits there. Trust that does nothing was never quite trust. Obedience is that trust getting up on its feet.

But how is that any different from just doing what you’re told because you’re scared of what happens if you don’t?

By where it starts, and by what it will pay. Fear obeys to stay safe, so it does the least it can and quietly keeps back the rest, which is exactly the religion God once turned down, “to obey is better than sacrifice” 1 Samuel 15:22. Saul saved the best animals for a grand offering and skipped the plain instruction, and the show fooled no one. Love runs the other way. The clearest picture scripture gives of it is Jesus himself, “becoming obedient to the point of death” Philippians 2:8, and no one forced his hand. He trusted his Father clear into the worst of it. Fear obeys with one eye on the exit. Love obeys with both hands, because it truly believes the one it follows is good.
