Topic

Sadness

How scripture honors sadness, and the good it quietly grows.

Sadness is the ache that shows up when something matters and it isn’t right. It isn’t quite grief, which is tied to a loss you can name, and it isn’t despair, which has given up on things ever being different. Sadness is quieter and far more common than either, the plain weather of a heart that is paying attention to a world that is often sad. It is not a malfunction. It is closer to what love feels like when something is wrong.

We tend to treat it as a problem to solve as fast as possible. Cheer up. Look on the bright side. Count your blessings. There is a whole industry aimed at getting you to feel better by lunchtime, and a low hum of guilt if you can’t, as though being sad were a small failure of gratitude or faith. So we paper over it, or numb it, and quietly wonder what is wrong with us for still feeling low.

The Bible has a strikingly different attitude. It never treats sadness as a sin, and it flatly refuses to rush you out of it. It hands you a whole season for weeping, it gives you blunt, tearful words to pray, and it goes so far as to say that sorrow can be better for you than laughter, that it does something in a person a good mood never could. It even shows God himself in tears. Far from a thing to be ashamed of, sadness in scripture is honest, human, and strangely fruitful, the kind of ground good things grow up out of.

This study walks from the permission to be sad to the quiet work sadness can do, and the joy scripture insists is on the far side of it. It won’t hurry you and it won’t pretend you should already be fine. Read it slowly. Every verse below is a door. Touch one and it opens into the chapter it came from, so you can stand in the whole room a single line was taken from.

The study

Start with the permission most of us are never given: that there is a right and proper time to be sad, built into the shape of a life. Ecclesiastes lays out the seasons, and weeping is simply one of them, no more a failure than winter is.

Weeping and laughing sit side by side, each with its season, each belonging to a life lived under heaven, “a time for every purpose under heaven” Ecclesiastes 3:1. Sadness isn’t the intrusion. It’s part of the weather, and the ones caught in it are the ones Jesus singles out for a blessing, “Blessed are those who mourn, for they shall be comforted” Matthew 5:4.

And when it comes, scripture doesn’t hand you a brave face to put on. It hands you words. A full third of the Psalms are sad ones, and they don’t tidy the feeling up before bringing it to God.

This is in the prayer book. Nobody edited the tears out. And the God it’s addressed to is not above such things himself, but stood at a friend’s grave and openly wept, “Jesus wept” John 11:35. Sadness is not meant to be carried alone, either, but shared and sat with, “Weep with those who weep” Romans 12:15.

Then scripture says something almost no one would put on a greeting card. It claims sadness is not just permitted but, in a real way, better for you than its cheerful opposite.

Sorrow does something to a person that a good mood cannot, it deepens them, it wises them up, “The heart of the wise is in the house of mourning” Ecclesiastes 7:4. Constant distraction keeps a heart shallow. Sadness, faced honestly, does the opposite.

That said, scripture is not naive. It knows there is a kind of sadness that heals and a kind that just eats you alive, and it names the difference plainly.

The difference isn’t how much it hurts, it’s where it’s pointed. Sorrow turned toward God softens and restores you. Sorrow turned only in on itself curdles, “the sorrow of the world produces death” 2 Corinthians 7:10. The goal was never to stop feeling it, but to let it do its good work and not its ruin.

Which is why the Bible keeps reaching for the picture of a farmer. Your tears aren’t spilled and wasted. They’re planted, and planted things come up.

Weeping is the sowing, not the end of the story. The one who went out in tears is promised a return trip with his arms full, “come again with joy, carrying his sheaves” Psalm 126:6. Nothing wept in faith is lost. It is in the ground, doing what seed does.

And that is finally what scripture promises about your sadness. Not that it isn’t real, not that you should skip it, but that it is going somewhere, and where it is going is joy.

He doesn’t promise the sadness away. He promises it will turn, into a joy no one can take back, “no one will take your joy away from you” John 16:22. It’s the oldest pattern in the book, the long night with morning already on its way, “Weeping may stay for the night, but joy comes in the morning” Psalm 30:5.

The conversation

One way a conversation about sadness can go in Sojourn. The verses are live, the same doors as above.

Everyone keeps telling me to look on the bright side and count my blessings, and honestly it just makes me feel worse, and a little guilty for being sad at all. Is it actually wrong to just feel down sometimes?

Not even a little. Scripture never files sadness under sin, and it refuses to hurry you out of it. It hands you a whole season for it, no apology attached, “a time to weep” Ecclesiastes 3:4, and it goes further than most of us would dare, calling sorrow good for you in a way a good mood can’t be, “Sorrow is better than laughter” Ecclesiastes 7:3. The pressure to perform cheer isn’t from the Bible. Even Jesus, who had every reason for joy, stood at a friend’s grave and simply broke down, “Jesus wept” John 11:35. You don’t owe anyone a brave face.

But I also don’t want to just sit and wallow in it forever.

That’s a fair worry, and scripture draws the exact line you’re feeling for. There’s a sorrow that softens you and turns you back toward life, and a sorrow that only corrodes, and the difference is where it’s pointed, “godly sorrow produces repentance leading to salvation” 2 Corinthians 7:10. And sadness was never meant to be a place you move into for good. It’s meant to be planted, put into the ground like seed that comes up as something else, “Those who sow in tears will reap in joy” Psalm 126:5. Feeling it fully and being stuck in it are not the same thing.

Does it ever actually lift, though? Some days it feels permanent.

The Bible is honest that it can stay a long time, and just as sure that it doesn’t get the last word. The night is real, and so is the morning after it, “Weeping may stay for the night, but joy comes in the morning” Psalm 30:5. Jesus promised his friends the very sadness they were in would one day turn, not be denied or skipped, but turned, “your heart will rejoice, and no one will take your joy away from you” John 16:22. And the ones who mourn are the ones he calls blessed, precisely because comfort is on its way to them, “Blessed are those who mourn, for they shall be comforted” Matthew 5:4.

Again a little while, and you will see me.” 17Some of his disciples therefore said to one another, “What is this that he says to us, ‘A little while, and you won’t see me, and again a little while, and you will see me;’ and, ‘Because I go to the Father’?” 18They said therefore, “What is this that he says, ‘A little while’? We don’t know what he is saying.” 19Therefore Jesus perceived that they wanted to ask him, and he said to them, “Do you inquire among yourselves concerning this, that I said, ‘A little while, and you won’t see me, and again a little while, and you will see me’? 20Most certainly I tell you that you will weep and lament, but the world will rejoice. You will be sorrowful, but your sorrow will be turned into joy. 21A woman, when she gives birth, has sorrow because her time has come. But when she has delivered the child, she doesn’t remember the anguish any more, for the joy that a human being is born into the world. 22Therefore you now have sorrow, but I will see you again, and your heart will rejoice, and no one will take your joy away from you. 23“In that day you will ask me no questions. Most certainly I tell you, whatever you may ask of the Father in my name, he will give it to you. 24Until now, you have asked nothing in my name. Ask, and you will receive, that your joy may be made full. 25“I have spoken these things to you in figures of speech. But the time is coming when I will no more speak to you in figures of speech, but will tell you plainly about the Father. 26In that day you will ask in my name; and I don’t say to you that I will pray to the Father for you, 27for the Father himself loves you, because you have loved me, and have believed that I came from God. 28I came from the Father and have come into the world. Again, I leave the world and go to the Father.” 29His disciples said to him, “Behold, now you are speaking plainly, and using no figures of speech. 30Now we know that you know all things, and don’t need for anyone to question you. By this we believe that you came from God.” 31Jesus answered them, “Do you now believe? 32Behold, the time is coming, yes, and has now come, that you will be scattered, everyone to his own place, and you will leave me alone. Yet I am not alone, because the Father is with me. 33I have told you these things, that in me you may have peace. In the world you have trouble; but cheer up! I have overcome the world.”

You have kept me alive, that I should not go down to the pit. 4Sing praise to Yahweh, you saints of his. Give thanks to his holy name. 5For his anger is but for a moment. His favor is for a lifetime. Weeping may stay for the night, but joy comes in the morning. 6As for me, I said in my prosperity, “I shall never be moved.” 7You, Yahweh, when you favored me, made my mountain stand strong; but when you hid your face, I was troubled. 8I cried to you, Yahweh. I made supplication to the Lord: 9“What profit is there in my destruction, if I go down to the pit? Shall the dust praise you? Shall it declare your truth? 10Hear, Yahweh, and have mercy on me. Yahweh, be my helper.” 11You have turned my mourning into dancing for me. You have removed my sackcloth, and clothed me with gladness, 12to the end that my heart may sing praise to you, and not be silent. Yahweh my God, I will give thanks to you forever!
